## 3. The Product

I led the design of a Transport Management System (TMS) tailored to the needs of a last-mile logistics company. The platform centralizes route planning, delivery tracking, and incident reporting through a desktop app and one mobile app used by two user types (coordinators and drivers). The goal was to digitize operations, reduce delivery failures, and improve visibility and control across the logistics chain.

---

## 4. The Core Problem

**Headline:** Operations were fragmented and difficult to monitor.

**Description:** Delivery routes were manually assigned, events weren't logged in real time, and key metrics like fuel efficiency, delivery time, and failed attempts were nearly impossible to track. The company needed an end-to-end solution to unify planning, execution, and reporting for more efficient, transparent, and scalable logistics.

---

## 5. How I thought through it (narrative sections)

Section title in the app: **How I thought through it.** Each subsection is a step in the process. Short paragraphs, images where they add meaning.

### 5.1 Understanding user and business needs

To understand the current logistics process, I ran several workshops with internal stakeholders. These sessions let me map operations from onboarding to final delivery and returns.

I mapped the system using journey maps and user flows, identifying how admins, coordinators, and operators interacted with the platform and where friction existed.

Key UX challenges included:

- Fragmented communication between coordinators and operators
- Manual route creation prone to errors
- No real-time feedback from the field
- Limited visibility on delivery status and incidents

These insights defined early screen flows, user roles, and the logic behind key interactions and validations.

### 5.2 Define and ideate

Once the main pain points were clear, I worked with stakeholders and developers to define system requirements and user permissions.

I worked directly in high-fidelity in Figma, iterating quickly with the team and keeping alignment with the business logic. Outcomes included:

- A modular TMS platform with views for KPIs, tasks, routes, vehicles, users, and clients
- Internal tools for manual and automatic route generation
- Real-time status indicators for ongoing deliveries
- Task-based alerts to support coordination and incident response
- Mobile flows adapted to operators' daily routines and route context

I explored interaction patterns for map integration, delivery confirmation, and time tracking per stop.

To structure the experience, I created sitemaps for both the TMS and the delivery app, defining user flows by role and permissions. That helped align scope with business priorities while keeping the operator experience focused and intuitive.

### 5.3 Prototype and test

We moved from flows to high-fidelity prototypes, skipping wireframes to focus on clear, polished designs aligned with the design system. That made it easier to communicate the product vision and get early feedback from stakeholders.

The prototypes covered desktop and mobile views and simulated real user interactions. We could spot friction and improve the UI for clarity and ease of use, and align the team before development.

We ran real-world testing with actual route uploads and route generation to ensure the system handled deliveries efficiently. Drivers did check-ins, followed routes, reported incidents, and resolved tasks in the app. Iterative testing and feedback led to optimized routes and a refined product. To date, tests have confirmed that the system works reliably and meets operational needs.

### 5.4 Iterate and finalize

After validating core functionality in testing, we gathered feedback from operators, coordinators, and stakeholders to find improvement areas. That iteration fine-tuned workflows, improved UI clarity, and optimized interactions on mobile and desktop.

We kept consistency with the design system while adapting components for real-world scenarios. Final refinements included performance optimizations, error handling, and better notifications, all aimed at supporting smooth daily operations and reducing user friction.

### 5.5 Handoff and collaboration

I prepared detailed handoff documentation: design specs, component libraries, and interaction guidelines so development could start smoothly. Close collaboration with developers and product managers was essential to respect technical constraints and keep design integrity.

During development I joined regular check-ins to review progress, clarify questions, and adjust designs when needed. That communication helped deliver a polished product aligned with user needs and business goals.

## 7. What I learned

This project reinforced how important it is to work closely with stakeholders in B2B to truly understand complex business rules. It showed that iterating quickly on high-fidelity prototypes can work well when wireframes aren't feasible.

I also learned that seamless integration between desktop and mobile is critical for operational efficiency. Keeping communication open through handoff and development ensures the final product meets both user needs and technical requirements.
